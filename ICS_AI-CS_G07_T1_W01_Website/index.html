Explanation and examples

An AI system is a workflow: it takes an input, runs a process, and returns an output. What makes the process 'AI' is that it usually relies on a model trained on many examples to recognise patterns, rather than on rules a person wrote by hand. A classifier gives the SAME output for the same input - it is deterministic; only generative models that sample can vary. Either way a confident output can still be wrong - which is why every result is checked against the task and a trusted source (verifybefore-trust). This launch lesson is about surfacing what students already know and framing good questions, not building yet. Keep all tool use teacher-operated, account-free and free of personal data. Cognitive demand for this lesson: Understand/Analyse | DOK 1-2 | SOLO: Uni to Multistructural. This builds on the training-data work from Bridging Week 1 (AI Conceptual Understanding - explains how training data affects output quality and performance): Main Term 1 assumes students already know that a model's training data shapes its output, and this launch lesson gives that idea a workflow vocabulary - input, process and output.

Source activity

Hands-on activity guide Overview: The class opens the unit with a curated stimulus, then explores and sorts everyday examples into input, process and output before co-building the vocabulary wall and safe-use charter and previewing the signature activity. Grouping / timing: Pairs for the sort, whole class for the wall, charter and debrief. Tool: A teacher-operated board demo plus a pre-printed, curated card set (a 'simulated AI' paper routine). Account -free and no personal data - students never log in or enter names. Facilitation steps: 1. Watch the stimulus and note one thing you noticed and one thing you wonder. 2. Turn your wondering into at least two clear questions about AI as a workflow. 3. With your partner, sort each example card as input, process or output. 4. Write one reason for each card - why does it belong there? 5. Add one word and its meaning to the class vocabulary wall. 6. Propose one responsible-use rule for our safe-use charter. Unplugged fallback: The whole lesson already runs unplugged on paper cards and a board demo. If the demo tool is unavailable, describe one worked input-output example aloud and have pairs sort the printed cards - no device needed.
